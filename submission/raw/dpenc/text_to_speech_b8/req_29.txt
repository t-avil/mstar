The terms "democracy" and "republic" are often used interchangeably, but they have distinct meanings and can overlap in practice. Here's a breakdown of the key differences:

### 1. **Definition**
- **Democracy**: A system of government where the authority of the government is created and sustained by the people, typically through voting and participation. The term comes from the Greek *demos* (people) and *kratos* (rule).
- **Republic**: A form of government where the country is considered a "public matter" (res publica), not the private concern or property of rulers. The head of state is usually an elected or appointed official, not a hereditary monarch.

### 2. **Focus**
- **Democracy** emphasizes **popular sovereignty** and **majority rule**. It focuses on how decisions are made and who holds power.
- **Republic** emphasizes the **structure of government** and the **absence of monarchy**. It focuses on the nature of the state and the role of the head of state.
