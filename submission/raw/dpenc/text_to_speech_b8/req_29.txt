The terms "democracy" and "republic" are often used interchangeably, but they refer to different aspects of government and can overlap in practice. Here's a breakdown of the key differences:

### 1. **Definition and Core Principles**

- **Democracy**:
  - A system of government where the authority of the government is created and sustained by the people, typically through voting.
  - The term comes from the Greek *dēmokratia*, meaning "rule by the people."
  - In a democracy, the people have the power to make decisions, either directly (direct democracy) or through elected representatives (representative democracy).

- **Republic**:
  - A form of government where the country is considered a "public matter" (res publica in Latin), not the private concern or property of rulers.
  - The head of state is not a monarch and is usually elected (e.g., a president), rather than inherited.
  - Power is derived from the people, but the system may include checks and balances, separation of powers, and constitutional limits.
