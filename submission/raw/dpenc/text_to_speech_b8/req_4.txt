Climate change is primarily driven by human activities that increase the concentration of greenhouse gases (GHGs) in the Earth's atmosphere. The main causes include:

1. **Burning of Fossil Fuels**: The combustion of coal, oil, and natural gas for energy and transportation releases large amounts of carbon dioxide (CO₂), the most significant greenhouse gas contributing to global warming.

2. **Deforestation and Land Use Changes**: Trees absorb CO₂ from the atmosphere. When forests are cleared for agriculture, urban development, or logging, not only is this carbon sink lost, but the stored carbon is often released back into the atmosphere.

3. **Industrial Processes**: Manufacturing activities, such as cement production, release CO₂ and other GHGs. Additionally, industrial processes emit potent greenhouse gases like nitrous oxide (N₂O) and fluorinated gases.

4. **Agriculture**: Livestock farming produces methane (CH��), a potent greenhouse gas, through enteric fermentation (digestion in ruminants like cows). Rice cultivation also emits methane, while synthetic fertilizers release nitrous oxide.

5. **Waste Management**: Landfills generate methane as organic waste decomposes anaerobically. Improper waste treatment and disposal contribute to GHG emissions.
