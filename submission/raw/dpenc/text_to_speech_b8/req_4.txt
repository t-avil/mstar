Climate change is primarily driven by human activities that increase the concentration of greenhouse gases (GHGs) in the Earth's atmosphere. The main causes include:

1. **Burning of Fossil Fuels**: The combustion of coal, oil, and natural gas for energy and transportation releases large amounts of carbon dioxide (CO₂), the most significant greenhouse gas contributing to global warming.

2. **Deforestation and Land Use Changes**: Trees absorb CO₂ from the atmosphere. When forests are cleared for agriculture, logging, or urban development, not only is this carbon sink lost, but the stored carbon is often released back into the atmosphere.

3. **Industrial Processes**: Manufacturing activities, such as cement production, chemical processing, and metal smelting, emit CO₂ and other GHGs like methane (CH��) and nitrous oxide (N₂O).

4. **Agriculture**: Livestock farming produces methane through enteric fermentation (digestion in ruminants like cows). Rice paddies also emit methane, and the use of synthetic fertilizers releases nitrous oxide, a potent greenhouse gas.

5. **Waste Management**: Landfills generate methane as organic waste decomposes anaerobically. Wastewater treatment processes also emit GHGs.
