Great question! The terms *hypothesis* and *theory* are often used interchangeably in everyday language, but in science, they have distinct meanings:

### Hypothesis:
- A **hypothesis** is an **educated guess** or a **proposed explanation** for a phenomenon, based on prior knowledge or observation.
- It is a **testable and falsifiable** statement that can be investigated through experiments or observations.
- A hypothesis is usually the starting point of scientific inquiry.
- Example: *"If plants are exposed to more sunlight, then they will grow faster."*

### Theory:
- A **theory** is a **well-substantiated explanation** of some aspect of the natural world that is supported by a large body of evidence.
- It is developed after repeated testing and confirmation of hypotheses.
- A scientific theory explains *why* or *how* something happens and can make predictions about future observations.
- Example: The **theory of evolution** explains how species change over time through natural selection, supported by fossil records, genetics, and many other lines of evidence.

### Key Differences:
| Feature | Hypothesis | Theory |
|--------|------------|--------|
| **Stage** | Early stage of investigation | Well-established, supported by evidence
