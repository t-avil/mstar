The Magna Carta, signed in 1215 by King John of England, is one of the most important legal documents in history. Its significance lies in several key areas:

1. **Foundation of Constitutional Law**: The Magna Carta is often seen as the first step toward constitutional government. It established the principle that everyone, including the king, is subject to the law. This was revolutionary at a time when monarchs were considered above the law.

2. **Protection of Rights**: It laid the groundwork for individual rights and liberties. For example, it guaranteed the right to a fair trial and protection from unlawful imprisonment—principles that later influenced the development of habeas corpus and due process.

3. **Limitation of Royal Power**: The Magna Carta curtailed the arbitrary power of the king by requiring him to consult with his barons before imposing taxes. This was a precursor to the idea of parliamentary consent for taxation, a cornerstone of modern democratic systems.

4. **Influence on Legal Systems**: The document influenced the development of legal systems in England and beyond. It inspired later constitutional documents such as the English Bill of Rights (1689), the United States Constitution, and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
