The difference between **weather** and **climate** lies in the **time scale** and **scope** of the atmospheric conditions they describe:

1. **Weather**:
   - Refers to the **short-term** atmospheric conditions in a specific place at a specific time.
   - It includes variables like temperature, humidity, precipitation, wind speed, and cloud cover.
   - Weather can change **from hour to hour or day to day**.
   - Example: "It's raining today and the temperature is 15°C."

2. **Climate**:
   - Describes the **long-term average** of weather patterns in a region over a **long period**, typically 30 years or more.
   - It includes averages and variability of temperature, precipitation, humidity, and other atmospheric conditions.
   - Climate reflects the **typical** weather conditions of an area.
   - Example: "The Mediterranean region has a hot, dry summer and mild, wet winter climate."

**Analogy**:
- Weather is like your **daily mood**—it can change quickly.
- Climate is like your **overall personality**—it's more stable and reflects long-term tendencies.

In summary, **weather is what you get today; climate is what you expect over time
