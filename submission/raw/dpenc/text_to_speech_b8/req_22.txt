GPS (Global Positioning System) is a satellite-based navigation system that provides location and time information anywhere on Earth. It works by using a network of satellites orbiting the Earth, ground control stations, and GPS receivers. Here's a step-by-step explanation of how it works:

1. **Satellite Network**: The GPS system consists of at least 24 operational satellites (plus spares) orbiting the Earth at an altitude of about 20,200 kilometers (12,550 miles). These satellites are arranged in six orbital planes, ensuring that at least four satellites are visible from any point on Earth at any time.

2. **Signal Transmission**: Each satellite continuously transmits radio signals that include:
   - The satellite’s precise location (ephemeris data).
   - The exact time the signal was sent (using highly accurate atomic clocks onboard the satellites).
