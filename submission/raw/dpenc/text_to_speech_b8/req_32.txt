Black holes form primarily through the gravitational collapse of massive stars. Here's a step-by-step explanation of how this process typically occurs:

1. **Massive Star Lifecycle**: Black holes usually begin as stars with at least 20–25 times the mass of our Sun. These stars burn through their nuclear fuel (hydrogen and helium) much faster than smaller stars.

2. **End of Nuclear Fusion**: When the star exhausts its nuclear fuel, it can no longer produce the outward pressure needed to counteract the inward pull of gravity.

3. **Core Collapse**: Without this balance, the core of the star collapses under its own gravity. For very massive stars, this collapse is so intense that it overcomes all known forces, including neutron degeneracy pressure.

4. **Supernova Explosion**: The outer layers of the star are violently expelled in a supernova explosion, while the core continues to collapse.
