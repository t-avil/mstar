Vaccines work by training your immune system to recognize and fight specific pathogens, such as viruses or bacteria, without actually making you sick. Here's how they do it:

1. **Introduction of Antigens**: Vaccines contain harmless parts of the pathogen (like proteins or sugars) or a weakened or inactivated version of the whole pathogen. These components are called antigens.

2. **Immune System Activation**: When you get vaccinated, your immune system detects these antigens as foreign invaders. It responds by producing immune cells and antibodies specifically designed to target that pathogen.

3. **Memory Formation**: After the immune response, your body retains a "memory" of the pathogen. This means that if you're exposed to the real thing later, your immune system can respond much faster and more effectively.

4. **Prevention of Disease**: Because your immune system is already prepared, it can quickly neutralize the pathogen before it causes illness or reduces the severity of the disease.

There are different types of vaccines, including:
- **mRNA vaccines** (like Pfizer-BioNTech and Moderna): Deliver genetic instructions for making a harmless piece of the virus (like the spike protein), prompting an immune response.
- **Viral vector vaccines**
