The main causes of World War I can be summarized by the acronym **M.A.I.N.**, which stands for **Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, and Nationalism**. These factors created a volatile environment in Europe that ultimately led to the outbreak of war in 1914. Here's a breakdown of each:

1. **Militarism**:
   - European powers engaged in an arms race, especially between Britain and Germany.
   - Military leaders gained influence in government decisions.
   - Countries built up large standing armies and stockpiled weapons, making war seem more likely and acceptable.

2. **Alliances**:
   - Europe was divided into two major alliance systems:
     - The **Triple Entente**: France, Russia, and Britain.
     - The **Triple Alliance**: Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy (Italy later switched sides).
   - These alliances meant that a conflict between two countries could quickly escalate into a war involving multiple nations.

3. **Imperialism**:
   - European powers competed for colonies and global influence.
   - Tensions arose over control of territories in Africa, Asia, and the Pacific.
   - This competition fueled rivalries, especially between Britain and Germany.
