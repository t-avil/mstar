The **water cycle**, also known as the **hydrologic cycle**, is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. It is a natural process that ensures the distribution and recycling of water across the planet, supporting all forms of life. The cycle involves several key processes:

1. **Evaporation**:
   - Water from oceans, rivers, lakes, and other water bodies is heated by the sun and turns into water vapor (gas).
   - This vapor rises into the atmosphere.

2. **Transpiration**:
   - Plants absorb water from the soil through their roots and release it into the air as water vapor through small pores in their leaves.
   - This process contributes to the overall evaporation from land surfaces.

3. **Condensation**:
   - As water vapor rises and cools in the atmosphere, it changes back into tiny liquid droplets.
   - These droplets gather to form clouds.

4. **Precipitation**:
   - When the water droplets in clouds become too heavy, they fall back to the Earth as precipitation.
   - This can take the form of rain, snow, sleet, or hail, depending on temperature and atmospheric conditions.
