The water cycle, also known as the **hydrologic cycle**, is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. It is a natural process that ensures the distribution and recycling of water across the planet, supporting all forms of life. The cycle involves several key processes:

1. **Evaporation**:
   - Water from oceans, rivers, lakes, and other bodies of water is heated by the sun and turns into water vapor (gas).
   - Plants also release water vapor into the air through a process called **transpiration**.
   - Together, evaporation and transpiration are often referred to as **evapotranspiration**.

2. **Condensation**:
   - As water vapor rises into the cooler upper atmosphere, it cools down and changes back into tiny liquid droplets.
   - These droplets gather around small particles in the air (like dust) to form **clouds**.

3. **Precipitation**:
   - When the water droplets in clouds become too heavy to stay suspended, they fall back to the Earth as **precipitation**.
   - This can take the form of rain, snow, sleet, or hail, depending on temperature and atmospheric conditions.
