Great question! "Affect" and "effect" are often confused because they sound similar and are both used in similar contexts, but they have different grammatical roles and meanings.

Here’s a breakdown:

### 1. **Affect** (usually a verb)
- **Meaning**: To influence or make a change in something.
- **Example**:  
  - *The weather can affect your mood.*  
  - *The new policy will affect how we work.*

👉 Think of **"affect"** as something that **does** something — it's an **action**.

---

### 2. **Effect** (usually a noun)
- **Meaning**: The result or outcome of something.
- **Example**:  
  - *The effect of the new policy was positive.*  
  - *The medicine had a calming effect.*

👉 Think of **"effect"** as the **result** — it's what happens **after** something is done.

---

### Quick Tip:
Use the **"A for Action"** trick:
- **Affect** is an **action** (verb).
- **Effect** is the **end result** (noun).
