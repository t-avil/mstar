The main causes of World War I can be summarized by the acronym **M.A.I.N.S.**, which stands for:

1. **Militarism**:  
   European powers, especially Germany, Britain, and France, engaged in an intense arms race during the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Military strength was glorified, and nations built up large standing armies and navies. This created a climate of competition and suspicion, making war seem like a viable or even necessary option.

2. **Alliances**:  
   A complex web of military alliances divided Europe into two opposing camps:
   - The **Triple Entente**: France, Russia, and Britain.
   - The **Triple Alliance**: Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy (though Italy later switched sides).
   These alliances meant that a conflict between two countries could quickly escalate into a continent-wide war.

3. **Imperialism**:  
   European powers competed fiercely for colonies and global influence, particularly in Africa and Asia. This rivalry heightened tensions, especially between Britain and Germany, which was rapidly expanding its empire and challenging British dominance.
