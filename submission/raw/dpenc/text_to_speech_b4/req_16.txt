The human immune system is a complex network of cells, tissues, and organs that work together to defend the body against harmful pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites. It also helps eliminate abnormal or damaged cells, including cancer cells. The immune system can be broadly divided into two main parts: the **innate immune system** and the **adaptive immune system**.

### 1. Innate Immune System (First Line of Defense)
The innate immune system is the body’s immediate, non-specific defense mechanism. It responds quickly to invaders but does not adapt to specific pathogens.

**Key components:**
- **Physical barriers:** Skin and mucous membranes act as the first line of defense by blocking pathogens.
- **Secretions:** Saliva, tears, and mucus contain enzymes (like lysozyme) that can destroy microbes.
- **Phagocytes:** Cells like **neutrophils** and **macrophages** engulf and digest pathogens.
- **Natural Killer (NK) cells:** Destroy infected or cancerous cells.
- **Inflammatory response:** When tissues are injured or infected, blood vessels dilate, allowing immune cells to reach the site. This causes redness, heat, swelling, and pain.
