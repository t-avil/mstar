Great question! "Affect" and "effect" are often confused because they sound similar and have related meanings, but they serve different grammatical roles in English.

Here’s how to tell them apart:

### 1. **Affect** – Usually a **verb** (action)
- Means to **influence** or **impact** something.
- Example:  
  - *The weather can **affect** your mood.*  
  - *Lack of sleep **affects** concentration.*

👉 Tip: Think of "affect" as something that **does** something (action verb).

---

### 2. **Effect** – Usually a **noun** (thing)
- Means the **result** or **outcome** of something.
- Example:  
  - *The new policy had a positive **effect** on sales.*  
  - *The side **effects** of the medicine were severe.*

👉 Tip: Think of "effect" as something that is **produced** (noun).

---

### Quick Mnemonic:
- **A**ffect → **A**ction (verb)  
- **E**ffect → **E**nd result (noun)

---

### Exceptions:
Sometimes, "effect" can be used as a verb meaning "to
