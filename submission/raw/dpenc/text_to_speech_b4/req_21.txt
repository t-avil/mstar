Occam's Razor is a problem-solving principle that suggests that among competing hypotheses, the one with the fewest assumptions should be selected. In other words, when multiple explanations are possible for a phenomenon, the simplest one—requiring the fewest assumptions—is usually the best choice.

The principle is named after William of Ockham, a 14th-century English Franciscan friar and philosopher. The original Latin phrase is *"entia non sunt multiplicanda sine necessitate,"* which translates to *"entities should not be multiplied without necessity."*

### Key Points:
- **Simplicity over complexity**: Occam's Razor doesn't claim that the simplest explanation is always correct, but that it's preferable as a starting point.
- **Use in science and philosophy**: It's widely used in scientific reasoning, helping researchers avoid unnecessary complexity in theories.
- **Not a proof**: It's a heuristic, not a rule of logic or a guarantee of truth.
