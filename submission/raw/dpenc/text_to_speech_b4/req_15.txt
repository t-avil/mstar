The time complexity of **quicksort** depends on the choice of pivot and the distribution of the input data:

1. **Best Case**:  
   - **O(n log n)**  
   - Occurs when the pivot divides the array into two nearly equal halves at each step. This leads to a balanced recursion tree with depth log n and n work at each level.

2. **Average Case**:  
   - **O(n log n)**  
   - On average, the pivot splits the array into roughly equal parts, even with random pivot selection. This is the typical performance in practice.

3. **Worst Case**:  
   - **O(n²)**  
   - Happens when the pivot is consistently the smallest or largest element (e.g., already sorted array with poor pivot choice). This results in unbalanced partitions, leading to n levels of recursion with n, n-1, n-2, ... work.

### Summary:
- **Best/Average**: O(n log n)  
- **Worst**: O(n²)

Quicksort is often preferred in practice due to its **in-place sorting** and **good average-case performance**, despite its worst-case inefficiency.<|im_end|>
