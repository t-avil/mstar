The wind off the North Sea had a different quality tonight—thicker, hungrier. Elias Thorne adjusted the collar of his oilskin coat, the damp wool already clinging to his neck. He’d been tending the lantern at the top of Blackrock Point for twenty-three years, and he knew the sea’s moods like the lines on his own hands. But this… this felt like the calm before the storm that would swallow the horizon whole.

He climbed the spiral stairs, each iron step groaning beneath his boots. The great Fresnel lens stood sentinel in the lantern room, its prisms catching the last of the dying light and refracting it into a thousand shards of silver across the darkening water. Elias checked the clockwork mechanism, wound the mainspring with practiced hands, and lit the wick. The kerosene flame hissed to life, casting long, dancing shadows across the curved glass.

Outside, the sea was a churning expanse of bruised purple and slate gray. No birds called. No distant gulls. Just the wind and the endless, restless rhythm of the waves against the cliffs. Elias pulled his hat lower and peered through the glass. Nothing. Not even a fishing boat’s light on the horizon.
