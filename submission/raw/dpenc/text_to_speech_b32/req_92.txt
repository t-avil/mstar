The **Baader-Meinhof phenomenon**, also known as the **frequency illusion**, is a cognitive bias where something you've recently noticed or learned about suddenly seems to appear everywhere. This happens not because the thing has actually increased in frequency, but because your brain has become more attuned to it.

For example, if you learn a new word, you might start noticing it in books, conversations, and advertisements more often than before. Similarly, if you buy a new car, you might suddenly see the same model on the road everywhere.

The phenomenon occurs in two stages:

1. **Attention**: You notice something new or unusual.
2. **Confirmation**: Your brain starts actively looking for that thing, making it seem more common than it is.

It's a form of selective attention and is not related to any supernatural or paranormal causes. It's simply how our brains process and prioritize information.<|im_end|>
