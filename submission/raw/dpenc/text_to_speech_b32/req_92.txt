The **Baader-Meinhof phenomenon**, also known as the **frequency illusion**, is a cognitive bias where something you've recently noticed or learned about suddenly seems to appear everywhere. This isn't because the thing has actually become more common, but rather because your brain has become more attuned to it.

Here’s how it works:

1. **First exposure**: You encounter something new—like a word, a name, a car model, or a concept—for the first time.
2. **Increased attention**: After that initial exposure, your brain starts paying more attention to it, making you more likely to notice it in the future.
3. **Perceived frequency**: You begin to see or hear it repeatedly, creating the illusion that it's suddenly everywhere.

For example, if you learn a new word, you might start noticing it in books, conversations, and ads. Or if you buy a new car, you might suddenly see the same model everywhere.
