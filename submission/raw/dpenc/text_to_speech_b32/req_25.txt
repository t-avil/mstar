The five stages of grief are a model developed by psychiatrist Elisabeth Kübler-Ross in her 1969 book *On Death and Dying*. Originally intended to describe the emotional process people go through when facing their own terminal illness, the model has since been widely applied to various forms of loss, including the death of a loved one, divorce, job loss, or other significant life changes.

The five stages are:

1. **Denial**  
   This is the initial reaction to loss, where the individual may struggle to accept the reality of the situation. It acts as a defense mechanism to help buffer the immediate shock. For example, someone might say, "This can't be happening to me."

2. **Anger**  
   As the reality of the loss sets in, feelings of frustration and anger may emerge. This anger can be directed at oneself, others, or even the situation itself. It's a natural response to the sense of unfairness or helplessness.
