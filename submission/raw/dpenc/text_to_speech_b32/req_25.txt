The five stages of grief are a model developed by psychiatrist Elisabeth Kübler-Ross in her 1969 book *On Death and Dying*. These stages describe the emotional process that individuals may go through when coping with loss, particularly in the context of terminal illness or death. The five stages are:

1. **Denial** – This is the initial reaction to loss, where the individual may struggle to accept the reality of the situation. It serves as a defense mechanism to help buffer the immediate shock.

2. **Anger** – As the reality of the loss sets in, feelings of frustration and anger may emerge. This anger can be directed at oneself, others, or even the situation itself.

3. **Bargaining** – In this stage, individuals may try to negotiate or make deals (often with a higher power) in an attempt to postpone or reverse the loss. For example, "If I do this, maybe I can get more time."

4. **Depression** – This stage involves deep sadness and mourning. The individual may feel overwhelmed by the reality of the loss and experience symptoms of clinical depression.
