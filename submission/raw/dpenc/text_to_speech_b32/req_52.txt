A **sonnet** is a type of poem that originated in Italy during the 13th century and became especially popular in England during the Renaissance. It is traditionally composed of **14 lines**, written in **iambic pentameter** (a rhythmic pattern of five pairs of unstressed and stressed syllables per line). Sonnets are known for their structured form and emotional depth, often exploring themes like love, beauty, time, and mortality.

There are three main types of sonnets:

1. **Italian (or Petrarchan) Sonnet**:
   - Originated in Italy, named after the poet Petrarch.
   - Divided into two parts: an **octave** (8 lines) and a **sestet** (6 lines).
   - Rhyme scheme: typically **ABBAABBA CDECDE** or **ABBAABBA CDCDCD**.
   - The octave presents a problem or theme, and the sestet offers a resolution or reflection (this shift is called the *volta*, or turn).
