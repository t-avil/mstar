Empathy and sympathy are both emotional responses to another person's situation, but they differ in depth and nature:

1. **Empathy**:
   - **Definition**: Empathy is the ability to understand and share the feelings of another person. It involves putting yourself in someone else's shoes and experiencing their emotions as if they were your own.
   - **Types**:
     - **Cognitive Empathy**: Understanding another person's perspective or mental state.
     - **Emotional Empathy**: Sharing the emotional experience of another person.
     - **Compassionate Empathy**: Understanding and sharing emotions, coupled with a desire to help.
   - **Example**: If a friend loses a loved one, you not only recognize their sadness but also feel a sense of sorrow yourself, and you might want to offer support.

2. **Sympathy**:
   - **Definition**: Sympathy is feeling concern or sorrow for someone else's misfortune, but without necessarily sharing their emotional experience. It's more about acknowledging their pain from a distance.
   - **Example**: If a friend loses a loved one, you might say, "I'm so sorry for your loss," but you don't necessarily feel the same depth of grief.
