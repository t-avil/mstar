The **Fermi Paradox** is the apparent contradiction between the high probability of the existence of extraterrestrial civilizations in the universe and the lack of evidence for, or contact with, such civilizations.

It is named after the Italian-American physicist **Enrico Fermi**, who, during a lunchtime conversation in 1950, reportedly asked, *"Where is everybody?"* This question highlights the paradox: given the vast number of stars and planets in the universe, many of which are likely Earth-like and could support life, we should expect to see signs of intelligent life elsewhere — yet we haven't.

### Key Points of the Paradox:

1. **Vastness of the Universe**:
   - There are billions of galaxies, each with billions of stars.
   - Many of these stars have planets, including potentially habitable ones in the "Goldilocks zone" (where liquid water can exist).

2. **High Probability of Life**:
   - Life arose on Earth relatively quickly after conditions stabilized.
   - If life is common, then intelligent, technological civilizations should have evolved elsewhere.
