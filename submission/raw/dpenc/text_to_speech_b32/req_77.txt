The **Fermi Paradox** is the apparent contradiction between the high probability of extraterrestrial civilizations existing in the universe and the lack of evidence for, or contact with, such civilizations.

It is named after the Italian-American physicist **Enrico Fermi**, who, during a casual lunchtime conversation in 1950, reportedly asked, *"Where is everybody?"* — highlighting the puzzling absence of observable signs of intelligent life beyond Earth.

### Key Points of the Paradox:

1. **Vastness of the Universe**:
   - The observable universe contains hundreds of billions of galaxies, each with hundreds of billions of stars.
   - Many of these stars have planets, and a significant fraction may lie in the "habitable zone" where conditions could support life.

2. **High Probability of Life**:
   - Given the sheer number of stars and planets, even if the probability of life arising on any given planet is very low, the total number of life-bearing worlds could still be enormous.
   - If life evolves into intelligent, technological civilizations, some should have developed far earlier than humans.
