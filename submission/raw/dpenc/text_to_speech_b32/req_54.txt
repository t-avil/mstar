The U.S. federal government is divided into three branches, as established by the U.S. Constitution:

1. **Legislative Branch**: Responsible for making laws. It consists of the U.S. Congress, which is a bicameral legislature made up of two chambers:
   - The **Senate** (100 members, 2 from each state)
   - The **House of Representatives** (435 members, apportioned based on state population)

2. **Executive Branch**: Responsible for enforcing and implementing laws. It is headed by the **President of the United States**, who is also the Commander-in-Chief of the armed forces. The branch includes the Vice President, the Cabinet (heads of federal executive departments), and various federal agencies and departments.

3. **Judicial Branch**: Responsible for interpreting laws and ensuring they are applied fairly. It is headed by the **Supreme Court** and includes lower federal courts such as:
   - U.S. Courts of Appeals
   - U.S. District Courts
   - Specialized courts (e.g., Tax Court, Court of International Trade)

These three branches operate under a system of **checks and balances**, ensuring that no single branch becomes too powerful.<|im_end|>
