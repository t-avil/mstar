CRISPR (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats) is a revolutionary gene-editing technology that allows scientists to precisely modify DNA within living organisms. It is derived from a natural defense mechanism found in bacteria, which use CRISPR sequences and associated proteins (like Cas9) to recognize and cut the DNA of invading viruses.

### How CRISPR Works:

1. **Targeting the DNA:**
   - Scientists design a short RNA sequence called **guide RNA (gRNA)** that matches the specific DNA sequence they want to edit.
   - The gRNA is combined with a Cas protein (most commonly **Cas9**) to form a complex.

2. **Finding the Target:**
   - The CRISPR-Cas9 complex scans the genome until it finds a DNA sequence that matches the gRNA.
   - The matching sequence must also be adjacent to a short DNA sequence called a **PAM (Protospacer Adjacent Motif)**, which is required for Cas9 to bind.

3. **Cutting the DNA:**
   - Once the correct sequence is found, Cas9 cuts both strands of the DNA double helix at the target site.
