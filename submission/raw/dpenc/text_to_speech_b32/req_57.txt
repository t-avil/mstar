The **trolley problem** is a famous thought experiment in ethics and moral philosophy, first introduced by British philosopher Philippa Foot in 1967. It is used to explore the differences between various ethical theories, particularly **utilitarianism** and **deontological ethics**.

### Scenario:
Imagine you are standing next to a railway track. A runaway trolley (or train) is hurtling down the track toward five people who are tied up and unable to move. You are standing next to a lever that can divert the trolley onto a different track. However, on that second track, there is one person tied up as well.

You have two choices:
1. **Do nothing**: The trolley continues on its current path, killing the five people.
2. **Pull the lever**: The trolley is diverted to the second track, killing the one person instead.

### The Question:
**Which choice is morally right?**

---

### Philosophical Implications:

1. **Utilitarian Perspective**:
   - Focuses on maximizing overall happiness or minimizing harm.
   - Pulling the lever is the morally correct choice because it results in fewer deaths (1 instead of 5).
   - The value is in the outcome: saving more lives is better.
