The **trolley problem** is a famous ethical thought experiment in philosophy, particularly in the field of moral psychology and ethics. It presents a hypothetical scenario designed to explore how people make moral decisions, especially in situations involving harm and sacrifice.

### The Scenario:
Imagine you are standing next to a railway track. A runaway trolley (or train) is hurtling down the track toward five people who are tied up and unable to move. You are standing next to a lever that can divert the trolley onto a different track. However, on that second track, there is one person tied up, also unable to move.

You have two choices:
1. **Do nothing** — the trolley continues on its current track, killing the five people.
2. **Pull the lever** — the trolley is diverted to the second track, killing the one person instead.

### The Moral Question:
Which choice is morally right? Is it better to sacrifice one life to save five, or is it wrong to actively cause harm (pulling the lever) even if it results in fewer deaths?
