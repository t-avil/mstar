Net neutrality is the principle that all internet traffic should be treated equally by internet service providers (ISPs), without discrimination or differential pricing based on user, content, website, platform, application, type of attached equipment, or method of communication. This means that ISPs should not block, throttle (slow down), or prioritize certain types of data or websites over others.

Key aspects of net neutrality include:

1. **No Blocking**: ISPs cannot block access to legal content, applications, services, or non-harmful devices.
2. **No Throttling**: ISPs cannot slow down specific internet traffic based on content, application, or service.
3. **No Paid Prioritization**: ISPs cannot create "fast lanes" for companies that pay extra, which would give them an unfair advantage over competitors.

The goal of net neutrality is to ensure an open and fair internet where innovation, free expression, and competition can thrive. It supports the idea that the internet should be a level playing field, allowing startups and small businesses to compete with larger corporations on equal footing.
