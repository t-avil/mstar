The Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle is a fundamental concept in quantum mechanics, formulated by German physicist Werner Heisenberg in 1927. It states that certain pairs of physical properties, such as position and momentum, cannot both be known with arbitrary precision at the same time. The more precisely one property is measured, the less precisely the other can be known.

### Significance of the Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle:

1. **Limits of Measurement**: It establishes a fundamental limit on the precision with which certain pairs of physical properties can be simultaneously known. This is not due to technological limitations but is an inherent feature of quantum systems.

2. **Wave-Particle Duality**: The principle reflects the dual nature of quantum objects. Particles like electrons exhibit both particle-like and wave-like behavior. A particle with a well-defined position is described by a localized wave packet, which requires a broad range of momenta (wavelengths), and vice versa.
