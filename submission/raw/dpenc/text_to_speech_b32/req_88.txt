The **Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle** is one of the foundational concepts in quantum mechanics, formulated by German physicist **Werner Heisenberg** in 1927. It fundamentally changed our understanding of the physical world by showing that there are inherent limits to how precisely certain pairs of physical properties of a particle can be known simultaneously.

### Core Idea:
The principle states that **it is impossible to simultaneously know both the exact position and the exact momentum (mass × velocity) of a particle**. The more precisely one property is measured, the less precisely the other can be known.

Mathematically, this is expressed as:

\[
\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \geq \frac{\hbar}{2}
\]

Where:
- \(\Delta x\) is the uncertainty in position,
- \(\Delta p\) is the uncertainty in momentum,
- \(\hbar\) is the reduced Planck's constant (\(\hbar = h / 2\pi\)).
