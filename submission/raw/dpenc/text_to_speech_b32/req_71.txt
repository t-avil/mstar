The scientific method is a systematic approach used to investigate phenomena, acquire new knowledge, or correct and integrate previous knowledge. It typically involves the following steps:

1. **Observation**: The process begins with observing a phenomenon or a set of events in the natural world. These observations may be qualitative (descriptive) or quantitative (numerical).

2. **Question**: Based on the observation, a specific question is formulated. This question aims to understand the cause or mechanism behind the observed phenomenon.

3. **Hypothesis**: A hypothesis is a testable and falsifiable explanation or prediction that attempts to answer the question. It is often stated as an "if-then" statement, such as "If X happens, then Y will occur."

4. **Experimentation**: An experiment is designed and conducted to test the hypothesis. This involves manipulating one or more variables (independent variables) and measuring the effect on other variables (dependent variables), while keeping all other conditions constant (controlled variables).

5. **Data Collection**: During the experiment, data is systematically recorded. This may include measurements, observations, or other relevant information.

6. **Analysis**: The collected data is analyzed using statistical or logical methods to determine whether it supports or contradicts the hypothesis.
