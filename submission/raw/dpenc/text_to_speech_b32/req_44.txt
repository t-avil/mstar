**Moore's Law** is an observation and prediction made by **Gordon Moore**, co-founder of Intel, in 1965. He noted that the number of transistors on a microchip doubles approximately every two years, leading to exponential growth in computing power.

### Key Points:
- **Original Observation**: In 1965, Moore observed that the number of components (like transistors) on integrated circuits had doubled every year, and he predicted this trend would continue for at least a decade.
- **Revised Prediction**: In 1975, Moore revised the timeline to **every two years**, which became the widely accepted version of "Moore's Law."
- **Impact**: This trend has driven rapid advancements in computing, enabling smaller, faster, and more affordable electronic devices.

### Important Notes:
- **Not a Physical Law**: Moore's Law is an empirical observation, not a scientific law. It reflects a trend in technological progress rather than a fundamental physical principle.
- **Challenges to Sustainability**: As transistors approach atomic scales, physical limitations (like quantum tunneling and heat dissipation) make it increasingly difficult to maintain this pace.
