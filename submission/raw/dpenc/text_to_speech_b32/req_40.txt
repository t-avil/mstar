Certainly! Here are three common logical fallacies:

1. **Ad Hominem**: This fallacy occurs when someone attacks the person making the argument rather than addressing the argument itself. For example, if someone dismisses a climate scientist's claims about global warming by saying, "He's just trying to get funding," they are committing an ad hominem fallacy. The scientist's credibility or motives are irrelevant to the validity of the evidence presented.

2. **Straw Man**: This fallacy involves misrepresenting or oversimplifying an opponent's argument to make it easier to attack. For instance, if Person A argues for stricter gun control laws to reduce gun violence, and Person B responds by saying, "So you want to take away everyone's guns and leave us defenseless," Person B has created a straw man. They've exaggerated and distorted the original argument to make it seem unreasonable.

3. **False Dilemma (or False Dichotomy)**: This fallacy presents only two options or outcomes when more exist. For example, saying, "You're either with us, or you're against us," ignores the possibility of neutral or alternative positions. This fallacy limits discussion by forcing a choice between extremes, even when other reasonable options may be available.
