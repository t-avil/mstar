The time complexity of **quicksort** depends on the choice of pivot and the distribution of the input data:

1. **Best Case**:  
   - **O(n log n)**  
   - Occurs when the pivot divides the array into two nearly equal halves at each step. This leads to a balanced recursion tree with depth log n, and each level takes O(n) time.

2. **Average Case**:  
   - **O(n log n)**  
   - On average, the pivot splits the array into roughly equal parts, leading to the same logarithmic depth and linear work per level.

3. **Worst Case**:  
   - **O(n²)**  
   - Happens when the pivot is consistently the smallest or largest element (e.g., already sorted array with poor pivot selection). This results in unbalanced partitions, leading to n levels of recursion, each requiring O(n) work.

### Summary:
- **Best/Average**: O(n log n)  
- **Worst**: O(n²)

Quicksort is often preferred in practice due to its excellent average-case performance and low overhead, despite its worst-case inefficiency.<|im_end|>
