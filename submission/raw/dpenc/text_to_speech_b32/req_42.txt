Bread rises during baking primarily due to the action of **yeast** and the **heat** from the oven. Here's a step-by-step explanation of how this happens:

1. **Yeast Fermentation (Before Baking):**  
   When you mix yeast with flour, water, and sugar, the yeast becomes active. It feeds on the sugars in the dough and converts them into **carbon dioxide (CO₂)** and **alcohol** through a process called fermentation. The CO₂ gas gets trapped in the gluten network of the dough, causing it to expand and rise. This is known as **proofing** or **rising**.

2. **Oven Spring (During Baking):**  
   When the dough is placed in a hot oven, several things happen:
   - The heat causes the **trapped CO₂ gas to expand**, making the bread rise even more.
   - The **yeast becomes very active** at first, producing a final burst of CO₂ before dying off due to the high temperature.
   - The **moisture in the dough turns to steam**, which also expands and contributes to the rise.
   - This initial rapid expansion is called **oven spring**.
