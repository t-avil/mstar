Dark matter is a form of matter that does not emit, absorb, or reflect light, making it invisible to electromagnetic radiation detection methods such as telescopes. Its presence is inferred from its gravitational effects on visible matter, radiation, and the large-scale structure of the universe.

Key points about dark matter:

1. **Gravitational Influence**: Although dark matter cannot be seen directly, its existence is supported by observations showing that galaxies rotate faster than expected based on the visible mass alone. This suggests the presence of additional, unseen mass—dark matter—that provides the extra gravity needed to hold galaxies together.

2. **Cosmic Structure Formation**: Dark matter plays a crucial role in the formation of galaxies and galaxy clusters. In the early universe, dark matter formed a "cosmic web" of gravitational scaffolding, around which ordinary matter (baryonic matter) collected to form stars and galaxies.

3. **Composition**: The exact nature of dark matter is still unknown. It is not composed of protons, neutrons, or electrons—the building blocks of ordinary matter. Leading candidates include Weakly Interacting Massive Particles (WIMPs), axions, and sterile neutrinos, but none have been definitively detected.

4. **Abundance**: Observations from the cosmic microwave background
