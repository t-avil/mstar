A microwave oven heats food using **microwave radiation**, a type of electromagnetic wave. Here's how it works:

1. **Generation of Microwaves**: Inside the microwave oven, a component called a **magnetron** converts electrical energy into microwave radiation, typically at a frequency of about **2.45 GHz**.

2. **Penetration into Food**: These microwaves penetrate into the food, reaching several centimeters deep. Unlike conventional ovens that heat from the outside in, microwaves heat the food more evenly throughout.

3. **Interaction with Water Molecules**: The key to microwave heating is the interaction between microwaves and **polar molecules**, especially **water molecules**, which are abundant in most foods. Water molecules have a positive end and a negative end (they are electric dipoles).

4. **Molecular Agitation**: The rapidly oscillating electric field of the microwaves causes these polar molecules to rotate back and forth billions of times per second. This rapid movement creates **molecular friction**, which generates **heat** within the food.

5. **Heat Distribution**: The heat generated by this molecular motion spreads through the food by conduction, cooking it from the inside out as well as the outside in.
