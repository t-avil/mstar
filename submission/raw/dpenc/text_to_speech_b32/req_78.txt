A microwave oven heats food using **microwave radiation**, a type of electromagnetic wave. Here's how it works:

1. **Microwave Generation**: Inside the microwave oven, a component called a **magnetron** converts electrical energy into microwave radiation, typically at a frequency of about **2.45 GHz**.

2. **Penetration into Food**: These microwaves penetrate into the food, reaching several centimeters deep, depending on the food's composition.

3. **Interaction with Water Molecules**: The key to heating is the interaction between microwaves and **polar molecules**, especially **water molecules**, which are abundant in most foods. Water molecules have a positive end and a negative end (they are electric dipoles).

4. **Molecular Agitation**: The rapidly oscillating electric field of the microwaves causes these polar molecules to rotate back and forth billions of times per second. This rapid rotation creates **molecular friction** and **collisions** between molecules.

5. **Heat Generation**: The kinetic energy from this molecular motion is converted into **thermal energy (heat)**, which cooks or warms the food from the inside out.
