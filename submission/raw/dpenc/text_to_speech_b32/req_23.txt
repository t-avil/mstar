Great question! "Affect" and "effect" are often confused because they sound similar and are both used in similar contexts, but they have distinct meanings and uses.

Here’s a breakdown:

### Affect (usually a verb)
- **Meaning**: To influence or produce a change in something.
- **Example**: "The weather can **affect** your mood."
- **Note**: "Affect" is most commonly used as a verb meaning to influence or impact.

> �� Tip: Think of "affect" as something that **acts upon** something else.

---

### Effect (usually a noun)
- **Meaning**: The result or outcome of a change.
- **Example**: "The new policy had a positive **effect** on employee morale."
- **Note**: "Effect" is most commonly used as a noun meaning the result or consequence.

> �� Tip: Think of "effect" as the **result** of an action.

---

### Quick Summary:
| Word     | Part of Speech | Meaning                     | Example |
|----------|----------------|-----------------------------|--------|
| Affect   | Verb           | To influence or impact      | "The cold weather **affected** my ability to focus." |
| Effect   | Noun
