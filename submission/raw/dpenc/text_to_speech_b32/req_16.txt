The human immune system is a complex network of cells, tissues, and organs that work together to defend the body against harmful pathogens such as bacteria, viruses, fungi, and parasites, as well as abnormal cells like cancer. It can be broadly divided into two main parts: the **innate immune system** and the **adaptive immune system**.

### 1. **Innate Immune System (First Line of Defense)**
This is the body’s immediate, non-specific response to pathogens. It acts within minutes to hours of infection and includes:

- **Physical Barriers**: Skin and mucous membranes (e.g., in the nose, mouth, and gut) prevent pathogens from entering the body.
- **Cellular Defenses**:
  - **Phagocytes** (e.g., macrophages, neutrophils): These cells engulf and destroy pathogens.
  - **Natural Killer (NK) Cells**: Target and destroy infected or cancerous cells.
- **Chemical Defenses**:
  - **Inflammatory Response**: When tissues are damaged or infected, the body releases chemicals that increase blood flow, attract immune cells, and promote healing.
  - **Fever**: Elevated body temperature can inhibit pathogen growth and enhance immune function.
