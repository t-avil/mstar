Climate change is primarily driven by human activities that increase the concentration of greenhouse gases (GHGs) in the Earth's atmosphere. The main causes include:

1. **Burning of Fossil Fuels**: The combustion of coal, oil, and natural gas for energy and transportation releases large amounts of carbon dioxide (CO₂), the most significant greenhouse gas contributing to global warming.

2. **Deforestation and Land Use Changes**: Trees absorb CO₂ from the atmosphere. When forests are cleared or burned, not only is this carbon sink lost, but stored carbon is also released back into the atmosphere.

3. **Agriculture**: Livestock farming produces methane (CH��), a potent greenhouse gas. Additionally, the use of synthetic fertilizers releases nitrous oxide (N₂O), another powerful GHG.

4. **Industrial Processes**: Manufacturing activities, such as cement production and chemical processing, emit CO₂ and other GHGs directly into the atmosphere.

5. **Waste Management**: Landfills and wastewater treatment release methane and CO₂ as organic waste decomposes anaerobically.

6. **Transportation**: Cars, trucks, ships, and airplanes rely heavily on fossil fuels, contributing significantly to CO₂ emissions.
