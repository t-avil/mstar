Climate change is primarily driven by human activities that increase the concentration of greenhouse gases (GHGs) in the Earth's atmosphere. The main causes include:

1. **Burning of Fossil Fuels**: The combustion of coal, oil, and natural gas for energy and transportation releases large amounts of carbon dioxide (CO₂), the most significant greenhouse gas contributing to global warming.

2. **Deforestation and Land Use Changes**: Trees absorb CO₂ from the atmosphere. When forests are cleared or burned, not only is this carbon sink lost, but stored carbon is also released into the atmosphere, increasing GHG levels.

3. **Agriculture**: Agricultural practices contribute to climate change through:
   - Methane (CH��) emissions from livestock (especially cattle) during digestion (enteric fermentation).
   - Nitrous oxide (N₂O) emissions from the use of synthetic fertilizers and manure management.
   - Land conversion for farming, which reduces carbon sequestration.

4. **Industrial Processes**: Manufacturing, cement production, and chemical processing release CO₂ and other GHGs directly into the atmosphere.

5. **Waste Management**: Landfills produce methane as organic waste decomposes anaerobically. Improper waste handling also contributes to emissions.
