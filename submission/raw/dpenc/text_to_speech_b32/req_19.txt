Dehydration occurs when your body loses more fluid than it takes in, disrupting normal bodily functions. Symptoms can range from mild to severe and may include:

**Mild to Moderate Dehydration:**
- Thirst
- Dry mouth and dry skin
- Decreased urine output or dark yellow urine
- Fatigue or weakness
- Dizziness or lightheadedness
- Headache
- Dry eyes or reduced tear production
- Muscle cramps

**Severe Dehydration:**
- Very dry skin and mucous membranes
- Rapid heartbeat and low blood pressure
- Rapid, shallow breathing
- Sunken eyes
- Low urine output or no urine for several hours
- Confusion or irritability
- Fainting or loss of consciousness
- In infants: sunken soft spot (fontanelle), no tears when crying, dry mouth, and fewer wet diapers

**Infants and Young Children:**
- No tears when crying
- Dry mouth and lips
- Sunken eyes or cheeks
- No wet diapers for 3 or more hours
- Lethargy or irritability
- High fever

**Older Adults:**
- May not feel thirsty even when dehydrated
