The Magna Carta, signed in 1215 by King John of England, is one of the most important legal documents in history. Its significance lies in several key areas:

1. **Foundation of Constitutional Law**: The Magna Carta is often seen as the first step toward constitutional government. It established the principle that everyone, including the king, is subject to the law. This was revolutionary at a time when monarchs were considered above the law.

2. **Protection of Rights**: It guaranteed certain rights and liberties to the barons (the nobility), including protection from illegal imprisonment, access to swift justice, and limits on arbitrary taxation. While initially limited in scope, these ideas evolved over time to influence broader civil rights.

3. **Rule of Law**: The Magna Carta introduced the idea that justice should not be sold, denied, or delayed. This concept became a cornerstone of modern legal systems.

4. **Influence on Future Documents**: The principles of the Magna Carta influenced many later legal and political developments, including the English Bill of Rights (1689), the United States Constitution, and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.
