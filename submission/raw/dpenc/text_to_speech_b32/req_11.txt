The main causes of World War I can be summarized by the acronym **M.A.N.I.A.**, which stands for:

1. **Militarism** – The belief that a strong military is essential for national strength and security. European powers, especially Germany, Britain, and France, engaged in an arms race, building up their armies and navies. This created a climate of tension and competition, making war seem more likely.

2. **Alliances** – A complex web of military alliances divided Europe into two opposing camps:
   - The **Triple Entente**: France, Russia, and Britain.
   - The **Triple Alliance**: Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy (Italy later switched sides).
   These alliances meant that a conflict between two countries could quickly escalate into a continent-wide war.

3. **Nationalism** – Intense pride in one’s nation led to aggressive foreign policies and competition among European powers. In the Balkans, nationalism fueled tensions between Slavic peoples and the Austro-Hungarian Empire, contributing to instability.

4. **Imperialism** – Competition among European powers for colonies and global influence heightened rivalries. Disputes over territories in Africa and Asia added to the tensions.
