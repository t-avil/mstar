The fall of the Western Roman Empire in 476 AD was not due to a single cause, but rather the result of a complex combination of internal and external factors that accumulated over centuries. Historians generally agree that it was a gradual process involving political instability, economic decline, military challenges, and social changes. Key factors include:

1. **Political Instability and Corruption**: The Roman Empire experienced frequent changes in leadership, with many emperors rising and falling quickly, often through assassination or civil war. This weakened central authority and made effective governance difficult. Corruption and inefficiency in the bureaucracy also undermined the state.

2. **Economic Troubles**: The empire faced severe economic problems, including inflation, heavy taxation, and a shrinking tax base. The reliance on slave labor discouraged innovation and productivity. Additionally, the debasement of currency (reducing the precious metal content in coins) led to inflation and loss of confidence in the monetary system.

3. **Military Challenges and Overextension**: The Roman military was stretched thin defending vast borders. As the empire expanded, it became harder to defend and administer. The army also became increasingly reliant on foreign mercenaries (foederati), who were less loyal to Rome and more focused on their own interests.
