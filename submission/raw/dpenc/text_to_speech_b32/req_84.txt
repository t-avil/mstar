The fall of the Western Roman Empire in 476 AD was the result of a complex interplay of internal and external factors that accumulated over centuries. While there is no single cause, historians generally agree that a combination of the following contributed to its decline:

1. **Political Instability and Corruption**:
   - The Roman Empire experienced frequent changes in leadership, with many emperors rising and falling quickly, often through assassination or civil war.
   - The Praetorian Guard, originally meant to protect the emperor, became a powerful political force that could install or remove emperors for their own benefit.
   - Corruption and inefficiency in government weakened central authority and reduced public trust.

2. **Economic Troubles**:
   - Heavy taxation to support the military and bureaucracy placed a burden on the lower classes.
   - Inflation and debasement of currency (reducing the silver content in coins) eroded economic stability.
   - The economy became increasingly dependent on slave labor, which discouraged innovation and technological advancement.
   - Decline in trade and urbanization as the empire fragmented.

3. **Military Challenges**:
   - The Roman military became increasingly reliant on mercenaries and non-Roman soldiers (especially Germanic tribes), who were less loyal to Rome
