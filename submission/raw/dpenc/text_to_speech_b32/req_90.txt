Great question! While the terms **mutation** and **genetic disorder** are related, they refer to different things:

### �� Mutation:
A **mutation** is a change in the DNA sequence of an organism. This can happen in a single nucleotide (point mutation), a larger segment of DNA, or even involve entire chromosomes. Mutations can occur naturally during DNA replication or be caused by environmental factors like radiation or chemicals.

- **Types of mutations**:
  - **Point mutations**: Change in one base pair (e.g., sickle cell anemia).
  - **Insertions/Deletions**: Adding or removing bases.
  - **Chromosomal mutations**: Large-scale changes like duplications, inversions, or translocations.

Mutations can be:
- **Harmful** (cause disease)
- **Beneficial** (provide evolutionary advantage)
- **Neutral** (no effect on function)

👉 **Important**: Not all mutations cause disease. Many are harmless or have no noticeable effect.

---

### �� Genetic Disorder:
A **genetic disorder** is a disease or condition caused by abnormalities in an individual’s DNA. These abnormalities may include:
- Mutations in specific genes
- Chromosomal abnormalities (e.g., Down syndrome)
