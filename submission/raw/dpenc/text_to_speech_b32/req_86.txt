Deductive and inductive reasoning are two fundamental approaches to logical thinking, differing primarily in the direction of their conclusions and the certainty of those conclusions.

### Deductive Reasoning:
- **Definition**: Deductive reasoning starts with general premises and moves toward a specific, logically certain conclusion.
- **Structure**: If the premises are true and the logic is valid, the conclusion **must** be true.
- **Example**:
  - Premise 1: All humans are mortal.
  - Premise 2: Socrates is a human.
  - Conclusion: Therefore, Socrates is mortal.
- **Key Features**:
  - **Validity**: A deductive argument is valid if the conclusion logically follows from the premises.
  - **Soundness**: A deductive argument is sound if it is valid and all premises are actually true.
  - **Certainty**: If the premises are true, the conclusion is guaranteed to be true.
