Anesthesia works by temporarily blocking pain and other sensations during medical procedures. It involves a combination of drugs that affect the central nervous system, particularly the brain and spinal cord, to induce a state of unconsciousness, pain relief, muscle relaxation, and amnesia. There are several types of anesthesia, each with different mechanisms:

1. **General Anesthesia**: This induces a reversible state of unconsciousness. It affects the entire body and is typically used for major surgeries. General anesthetics work by enhancing the activity of inhibitory neurotransmitters (like GABA) and inhibiting excitatory ones (like glutamate), which reduces neuronal activity in the brain. This leads to a loss of consciousness, pain perception, and memory. Common agents include propofol, sevoflurane, and isoflurane.

2. **Regional Anesthesia**: This numbs a large area of the body, such as an entire limb or half of the body. It includes spinal and epidural anesthesia, which involve injecting anesthetic into the cerebrospinal fluid or surrounding tissues to block nerve signals. These work by inhibiting sodium channels in nerve cells, preventing the transmission of pain signals.
