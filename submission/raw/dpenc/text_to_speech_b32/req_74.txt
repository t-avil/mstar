A search engine indexes the web through a multi-step process involving crawling, parsing, storing, and ranking. Here's a simplified breakdown of how it works:

1. **Crawling**:
   - Search engines use automated programs called **crawlers** or **spiders** to discover web pages.
   - Crawlers start with a list of URLs (often from previous crawls or sitemaps) and follow links from one page to another.
   - They continuously explore the web, visiting new pages and updating their list of known URLs.

2. **Fetching**:
   - Once a crawler finds a URL, it retrieves the page's content (HTML, images, scripts, etc.) from the server.
   - This is similar to how your browser loads a webpage.

3. **Parsing**:
   - The search engine analyzes the fetched content to understand what the page is about.
   - It extracts text, identifies keywords, parses metadata (like titles and descriptions), and follows links to discover new pages.
   - It also processes structured data (e.g., schema markup) and media files.

4. **Indexing**:
   - The parsed information is stored in a massive database called the **index**.
   - The index is optimized for fast retrieval
