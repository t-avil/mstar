A search engine indexes the web through a multi-step process that involves crawling, parsing, storing, and ranking web pages. Here's a breakdown of how it works:

1. **Crawling**:
   - Search engines use automated programs called **crawlers** or **spiders** to discover web pages.
   - Crawlers start with a list of URLs (often from previous crawls or sitemaps) and follow links from one page to another.
   - They continuously explore the web, visiting millions of pages daily.
   - Crawlers respect the `robots.txt` file, which tells them which parts of a site they can or cannot access.

2. **Parsing and Rendering**:
   - Once a page is crawled, the search engine parses its content.
   - It extracts text, images, metadata (like titles and descriptions), and links.
   - Modern crawlers also render JavaScript-heavy pages to understand content that loads dynamically.

3. **Indexing**:
   - The parsed content is stored in a massive database called the **index**.
   - The index is optimized for fast retrieval and contains information about each page, such as:
     - Keywords and phrases
     - Page structure (headings, paragraphs, etc.)
     - Links to
