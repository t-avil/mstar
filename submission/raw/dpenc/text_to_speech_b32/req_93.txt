An airplane generates lift primarily through the shape of its wings and the movement of air over and under them. This process is explained by the **principles of aerodynamics**, particularly **Bernoulli's principle** and **Newton's third law of motion**.

Here’s how it works:

1. **Airfoil Shape**: The wings of an airplane are designed with an **airfoil shape**—curved on the top and flatter on the bottom. This shape causes air to move faster over the top surface than the bottom.

2. **Bernoulli’s Principle**: According to this principle, faster-moving air has lower pressure. So, the faster-moving air above the wing creates **lower pressure** compared to the slower-moving air below the wing. This pressure difference results in an upward force called **lift**.

3. **Newton’s Third Law**: As the wing pushes air downward (due to its angle of attack), the air pushes back upward on the wing. This reaction force also contributes to lift.

4. **Angle of Attack**: The angle at which the wing meets the oncoming air (angle of attack) affects lift. Increasing the angle increases lift up to a point, but too much can cause airflow separation and lead to a stall.
