An airplane generates lift primarily through the shape of its wings and the movement of air over and under them. This process is explained by **Bernoulli's principle** and **Newton's third law of motion**. Here's how it works:

1. **Airfoil Shape**: The wings of an airplane are designed with a specific shape called an **airfoil**. The top surface of the wing is curved, while the bottom is relatively flat. This shape causes air to move faster over the top of the wing than underneath.

2. **Bernoulli's Principle**: According to this principle, as the speed of a fluid (like air) increases, its pressure decreases. Because air moves faster over the curved top surface, the pressure above the wing is lower than the pressure below it. This pressure difference creates an upward force called **lift**.

3. **Newton's Third Law**: As the wing pushes air downward (due to its angle of attack and shape), the air pushes back upward on the wing with an equal and opposite force. This is another component of lift.
