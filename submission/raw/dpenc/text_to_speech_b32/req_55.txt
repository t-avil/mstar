Wi-Fi is a technology that allows electronic devices to connect to a wireless local area network (WLAN) using radio waves. Here's a simplified explanation of how it works:

1. **Wireless Router**: At the center of a Wi-Fi network is a device called a wireless router. This router is connected to a modem, which receives the internet signal from your Internet Service Provider (ISP). The router then converts this signal into a wireless signal.

2. **Radio Waves**: The router broadcasts the internet signal using radio waves, typically in the 2.4 GHz or 5 GHz frequency bands. These radio waves can travel through the air and are picked up by devices equipped with Wi-Fi receivers, such as smartphones, laptops, tablets, and smart home devices.

3. **Wi-Fi Adapter**: Each device that wants to connect to the Wi-Fi network has a Wi-Fi adapter (also known as a wireless network interface card). This adapter receives the radio signals from the router and converts them into data that the device can understand.
