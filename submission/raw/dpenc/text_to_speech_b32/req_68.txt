The Rosetta Stone is a granodiorite stele discovered in 1799 near the Egyptian town of Rashid (Rosetta). It is of immense historical and linguistic significance because it provided the key to deciphering Egyptian hieroglyphs, which had been undecipherable for over 1,400 years.

The stone features the same text inscribed in three scripts:
1. **Ancient Greek** – a language scholars could still read.
2. **Demotic** – a cursive script used for everyday purposes in ancient Egypt.
3. **Egyptian hieroglyphs** – the formal script used for religious and monumental inscriptions.

Because the Greek text was already understood, scholars could use it as a reference to compare and decode the other two scripts. The breakthrough came in the early 19th century when French linguist **Jean-François Champollion** successfully deciphered the hieroglyphs in 1822 by recognizing that hieroglyphs were not purely symbolic but also phonetic, representing sounds and syllables.
