The sea was a bruised purple at dawn, the kind that only comes after a storm has passed and the sky is still bruised from the fight. Elias Thorne stood at the base of the stone tower, his breath pluming in the cold air, the salt spray clinging to his woolen coat like a second skin. He’d been the keeper of Blackwater Point for twenty-three years, longer than most men remembered the last time the foghorn had been silenced by a shipwreck. The light itself was a marvel of brass and glass, a great eye that swept the coast every ten seconds, but it was the silence between the sweeps that Elias knew best—the heavy, watchful quiet of the sea at night, the only sound the rhythmic groan of the waves against the rocks below.

He climbed the spiral stairs, each step worn smooth by generations of boots. The lantern room was warm, the great lens humming faintly as it turned. Elias adjusted the wick, checked the oil level, and then pulled out the logbook. The entry for that night was brief: *“Clear skies. No vessels reported.
