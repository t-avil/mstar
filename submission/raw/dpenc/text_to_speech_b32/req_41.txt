The mitochondrion is often referred to as the "powerhouse" of the cell because its primary role is to generate adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the main energy currency of the cell. This process occurs through cellular respiration, which involves several key steps:

1. **Glycolysis**: Although this process occurs in the cytoplasm, it produces pyruvate, which is transported into the mitochondrion for further processing.

2. **Pyruvate Decarboxylation**: Inside the mitochondrial matrix, pyruvate is converted into acetyl-CoA, which enters the citric acid cycle (also known as the Krebs cycle).

3. **Citric Acid Cycle (Krebs Cycle)**: This cycle takes place in the mitochondrial matrix and generates high-energy electron carriers (NADH and FADH₂) and a small amount of ATP.

4. **Electron Transport Chain (ETC) and Oxidative Phosphorylation**: Located in the inner mitochondrial membrane, the ETC uses electrons from NADH and FADH₂ to create a proton gradient across the membrane. This gradient drives ATP synthase to produce large amounts of ATP.
