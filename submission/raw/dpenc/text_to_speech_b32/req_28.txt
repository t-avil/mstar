Vaccines work by training your immune system to recognize and fight off specific pathogens, such as viruses or bacteria, without actually making you sick. Here's a step-by-step explanation of how they work:

1. **Introduction of Antigens**: Vaccines contain harmless parts of the pathogen (like proteins or sugars) or a weakened or inactivated version of the whole pathogen. These components are called antigens.

2. **Immune System Activation**: When you get vaccinated, your immune system detects these antigens as foreign invaders. This triggers an immune response, similar to what would happen if you were infected with the actual disease.

3. **Production of Immune Cells and Antibodies**: Your body produces specialized immune cells, including B cells and T cells. B cells create antibodies that can bind to the pathogen and neutralize it. T cells help destroy infected cells and coordinate the immune response.

4. **Memory Cell Formation**: After the immune response, some of the immune cells become memory cells. These cells "remember" the pathogen and can respond much faster and more effectively if you're exposed to it again in the future.
