The difference between **weather** and **climate** lies in the **time scale** and **scope** of the atmospheric conditions being described:

1. **Weather**:
   - Refers to the **short-term** atmospheric conditions in a specific place at a specific time.
   - It includes variables like temperature, humidity, precipitation, wind speed, and cloud cover.
   - Weather can change **from hour to hour or day to day**.
   - Example: "It is sunny and 25°C today, but it will rain tomorrow."

2. **Climate**:
   - Refers to the **long-term** average of weather patterns in a region over a **long period**, typically 30 years or more.
   - It describes typical conditions, such as average temperature, rainfall, and seasonal patterns.
   - Climate is more stable and predictable than weather.
   - Example: "The Mediterranean region has a hot, dry summer and mild, wet winter climate."

**Analogy**:
- Weather is like your **mood today**—it can change quickly.
- Climate is like your **personality**—it reflects your general tendencies over time.

In summary, **weather is what you get; climate is what you expect**.<|im_end|>
