Great question! **Copyright** and **trademark** are both forms of intellectual property protection, but they protect different types of creations and serve different purposes. Here's a breakdown of the key differences:

### 1. **What They Protect**
- **Copyright**: Protects **original creative works** that are fixed in a tangible medium. This includes:
  - Literary works (books, articles, poems)
  - Music and sound recordings
  - Artwork, photographs, and films
  - Software code
  - Dramatic works (plays, scripts)
  - Architectural designs

- **Trademark**: Protects **symbols, names, logos, slogans, or other identifiers** that distinguish the source of goods or services. This includes:
  - Brand names (e.g., "Nike")
  - Logos (e.g., the Nike swoosh)
  - Slogans (e.g., "Just Do It")
  - Sounds (e.g., the NBC chimes)
  - Colors or packaging (e.g., the distinctive red color of Coca-Cola bottles)

- **Copyright**: Prevents others from copying, distributing, or publicly performing your creative work without permission.
