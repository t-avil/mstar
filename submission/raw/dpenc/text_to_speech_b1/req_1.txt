Certainly! Machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) are both subfields of artificial intelligence (AI), but they differ in their approaches, architectures, and applications. Here's a breakdown of the key differences:

### 1. **Definition**
- **Machine Learning**: A broad field of AI where systems learn patterns from data and make decisions or predictions without being explicitly programmed. It uses algorithms that improve automatically through experience.
- **Deep Learning**: A subset of machine learning that uses artificial neural networks with multiple layers (deep architectures) to model complex patterns in data.

### 2. **Architecture**
- **Machine Learning**: Uses simpler models such as linear regression, decision trees, support vector machines (SVM), random forests, and k-nearest neighbors (KNN).
- **Deep Learning**: Uses deep neural networks (DNNs), including convolutional neural networks (CNNs), recurrent neural networks (RNNs), and transformers, with many layers (often 10+).

- **Machine Learning**: Requires significant manual feature engineering. Experts must identify and extract relevant features from raw data.
