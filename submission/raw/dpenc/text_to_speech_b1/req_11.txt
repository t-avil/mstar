The main causes of World War I can be summarized by the acronym **M.A.N.I.A.**, which stands for:

1. **Militarism**:  
   The arms race among European powers, especially between Germany and Britain, created a climate of competition and tension. Countries built up their military forces and developed war plans, making war seem more likely and acceptable.

2. **Alliances**:  
   Europe was divided into two major alliance systems:  
   - The **Triple Entente** (France, Russia, and Britain)  
   - The **Triple Alliance** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy)  
   These alliances meant that a conflict between two countries could quickly escalate into a war involving multiple nations.

3. **Nationalism**:  
   Intense national pride and competition among nations fueled tensions. In the Balkans, nationalism among Slavic peoples threatened the stability of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, leading to conflicts like the Bosnian Crisis and the assassination of Archduke Franz Ferdinand.

4. **Imperialism**:  
   Competition for colonies and global influence heightened rivalries, especially between Britain, France, and Germany. This competition contributed to diplomatic friction and mistrust.
