Great question! Both **similes** and **metaphors** are figures of speech used to make comparisons, but they do so in different ways:

### **Simile**
A simile **explicitly compares two things** using words like **"like"** or **"as."**

- **Example:**  
  *"Her smile is as bright as the sun."*  
  *"He runs like a cheetah."*

👉 The use of "like" or "as" makes it clear that the comparison is not literal.

---

### **Metaphor**
A metaphor **directly states that one thing is another**, without using "like" or "as." It implies the comparison more strongly.

- **Example:**  
  *"Time is a thief."*  
  *"The world is a stage."*

👉 This suggests that time *is* a thief (stealing moments), or that life *is* a theatrical performance.

---

### **Key Difference:**
| Feature | Simile | Metaphor |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **Comparison word** | Uses "like" or "as" | No "like" or "as" |
| **Directness** | More indirect | More direct and powerful |
