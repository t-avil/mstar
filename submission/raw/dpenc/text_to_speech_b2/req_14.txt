The wind off the North Sea had a different quality tonight—thicker, hungrier. Elias Thorne adjusted the collar of his oilskin coat, the damp wool already clinging to his neck. He’d been tending the lantern at the top of Blackrock Point for twenty-three years, and he knew the sea’s moods like the lines on his own hands. But this… this felt like the calm before the storm that would swallow the horizon whole.

He climbed the spiral stairs, each iron tread groaning under his weight, the rhythmic *clank-clank* of his boots echoing in the narrow tower. The air grew colder, sharper, as he ascended. At the lantern room, he paused, peering through the thick glass panes. The sea was a vast, ink-black expanse, broken only by the occasional whitecap catching the moonlight. But the horizon… the horizon was wrong. Too still. Too dark. Like something was holding its breath.
