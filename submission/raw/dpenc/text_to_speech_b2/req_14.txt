The sea was a bruised purple that night, the sky choked with low-hanging clouds that swallowed the stars whole. Inside the stone tower, Elias Thorne wiped a streak of salt spray from the great Fresnel lens with a cloth that had long since surrendered its color to the elements. The air hung thick with the scent of damp wool, old oil, and the faint, metallic tang of rust. Outside, the wind howled like a wounded thing, rattling the iron shutters and making the very foundations of the lighthouse groan in protest.

Elias was alone, as he had been for the past three years since the last keeper had vanished during a particularly violent storm. The island was small, a jagged tooth of rock jutting from the North Atlantic, and the lighthouse was its only sentinel. He knew every crack in the whitewashed walls, every creak of the spiral staircase, every shift in the wind’s moan. He knew the rhythm of the waves crashing against the rocks below – the deep, rolling thunder of the swell, the sharper *crack* as it shattered against the cliffs, the long, sighing retreat.
