The **Pythagorean theorem** is a fundamental principle in Euclidean geometry that applies to right triangles. It states:

> In a right triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides (legs).

Mathematically, if $ a $ and $ b $ are the lengths of the legs and $ c $ is the length of the hypotenuse, then:

$$
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
$$

This theorem is named after the ancient Greek mathematician **Pythagoras**, although it was known to earlier civilizations such as the Babylonians and Indians.

### Example:
If a right triangle has legs of lengths 3 and 4, then the hypotenuse $ c $ is:

$$
c = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5
$$

So, the sides are 3, 4, and 5 — a well-known Pythagorean triple.
