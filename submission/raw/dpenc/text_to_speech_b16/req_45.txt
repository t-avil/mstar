Natural selection is a fundamental mechanism of evolution, first articulated by Charles Darwin and Alfred Russel Wallace in the 19th century. It explains how species change over time and adapt to their environments through the differential survival and reproduction of individuals with advantageous traits.

Here’s a breakdown of the key components of natural selection:

1. **Variation**: Within any population, individuals exhibit variation in traits—such as size, color, speed, or resistance to disease. This variation arises from genetic differences due to mutations, gene recombination during sexual reproduction, and other sources.

2. **Overproduction and Struggle for Existence**: Most species produce more offspring than the environment can support. This leads to competition for limited resources like food, mates, and shelter. Not all offspring survive to reproduce.

3. **Differential Survival and Reproduction**: Individuals with traits that are better suited to their environment are more likely to survive and reproduce. These advantageous traits are called adaptations.

4. **Inheritance**: Traits that improve survival and reproduction are passed on to the next generation through genes. Over time, these beneficial traits become more common in the population.

5. **Evolution**: As advantageous traits accumulate over generations, the population gradually changes—this is evolution by natural selection.
