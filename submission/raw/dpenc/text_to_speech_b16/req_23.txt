Great question! "Affect" and "effect" are often confused because they sound similar and are both used in similar contexts, but they have different meanings and grammatical roles.

Here’s a breakdown:

### 1. **Affect** (usually a verb)
- **Meaning**: To influence or make a change in something.
- **Example**:  
  - *The weather can affect your mood.*  
  - *The new policy will affect how we work.*

👉 Think of "affect" as something that **does** something — it's an action.

---

### 2. **Effect** (usually a noun)
- **Meaning**: The result or outcome of something.
- **Example**:  
  - *The new policy had a positive effect on productivity.*  
  - *The side effects of the medicine were severe.*

👉 Think of "effect" as the **result** of an action.

---

### Mnemonic to remember:
- **A**ffect → **A**ction (verb)
- **E**ffect → **E**nd result (noun)

---

- **Effect** can be used as a verb meaning "to bring about" or "to cause" (less common).
