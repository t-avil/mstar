Great question! The terms *hypothesis* and *theory* are often used interchangeably in everyday language, but in science, they have distinct meanings:

### Hypothesis:
- A **testable and falsifiable** prediction or proposed explanation for a phenomenon.
- It is an **initial idea** based on observations or prior knowledge.
- It is usually **specific and narrow** in scope.
- It must be **tested through experiments or observations**.
- Example: "If plants are exposed to more sunlight, then they will grow faster."

### Theory:
- A **well-substantiated explanation** of some aspect of the natural world that is based on a body of evidence.
- It has been **extensively tested and supported** by multiple lines of evidence.
- It is **broad in scope** and can explain many related phenomena.
- It is **not just a guess**—it is a framework that has withstood rigorous testing.
- Example: The **Theory of Evolution** explains how species change over time through natural selection, supported by fossil records, genetics, and observations.

- A **hypothesis** is a starting point—a "what if?" question.
