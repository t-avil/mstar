Great question! The terms *hypothesis* and *theory* are often used interchangeably in everyday language, but in science, they have distinct meanings:

### Hypothesis:
- A **testable and falsifiable** prediction or proposed explanation for a phenomenon.
- It is an **initial idea** based on observations or prior knowledge.
- It is usually **specific and narrow** in scope.
- It must be **tested through experiments or observations**.
- Example: "If plants are exposed to more sunlight, then they will grow faster."

### Theory:
- A **well-substantiated explanation** of some aspect of the natural world that is supported by a large body of evidence.
- It is **broad in scope** and explains a wide range of phenomena.
- It has been **tested and confirmed repeatedly** through observation and experimentation.
- A scientific theory is **not just a guess**—it is a robust framework that explains *why* things happen.
- Example: The **Theory of Evolution** explains how species change over time through natural selection.

### Key Difference:
- A **hypothesis** is a starting point—a possible explanation that needs testing.
- A **theory** is the result of extensive testing and validation of many hypotheses.
