Quantum entanglement is a phenomenon in quantum physics where two or more particles become linked in such a way that the state of one particle is directly related to the state of the other(s), no matter how far apart they are. This connection persists even when the particles are separated by vast distances—sometimes across the universe.

Here are some key points about quantum entanglement:

1. **Linked States**: When particles are entangled, measuring the state of one (such as its spin, polarization, or momentum) instantly determines the state of the other(s), even if they are light-years apart.

2. **Non-locality**: This instantaneous correlation appears to violate classical intuitions about locality (the idea that objects are only influenced by their immediate surroundings). However, it does not allow for faster-than-light communication, as the outcome of measurements is random and cannot be controlled.

3. **Einstein's "Spooky Action at a Distance"**: Albert Einstein famously criticized entanglement, calling it "spooky action at a distance," because it challenged classical physics. However, experiments (like those testing Bell's inequalities) have confirmed that entanglement is real and consistent with quantum mechanics.
