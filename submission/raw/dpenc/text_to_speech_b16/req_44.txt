**Moore's Law** is an observation and prediction made by **Gordon Moore**, co-founder of Intel, in 1965. He noted that the number of transistors on a microchip doubles approximately every two years, leading to exponential growth in computing power.

### Key Points:
- **Original Observation**: In 1965, Moore observed that the number of components (like transistors) on integrated circuits had doubled every year. He later revised this to **every two years**.
- **Impact**: This trend has driven rapid advancements in computing, making devices faster, smaller, and more affordable over time.
- **Not a Physical Law**: Moore's Law is not a scientific law but rather an empirical observation and industry trend.
- **Current Status**: While the pace has slowed due to physical limitations (e.g., atomic-scale transistors), the semiconductor industry has adapted with innovations like 3D chip stacking, new materials (e.g., graphene), and specialized architectures (e.g., AI accelerators).

### Significance:
Moore's Law has shaped the tech industry, influencing everything from smartphones to cloud computing and artificial intelligence. It has also set expectations for innovation and performance improvements in electronics.
