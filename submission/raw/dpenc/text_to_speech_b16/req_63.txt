**Opportunity cost** is an economic concept that refers to the value of the next best alternative that you give up when making a decision. In other words, it's what you sacrifice when you choose one option over another.

### Key Points:
- **It's not just monetary**: Opportunity cost includes time, effort, and other resources.
- **It's about trade-offs**: Every choice involves giving up something else.
- **It's subjective**: The value of the "next best" alternative depends on the individual or organization making the decision.

### Example:
Imagine you have $100 and must choose between:
1. Buying a new pair of shoes.
2. Investing that $100 in the stock market.

If you choose the shoes, the **opportunity cost** is the potential return you could have earned from the investment. If you invest the money, the opportunity cost is the enjoyment or utility you would have gotten from the shoes.

### Why It Matters:
Understanding opportunity cost helps individuals and businesses make more informed decisions by considering not just the direct costs and benefits, but also what they're giving up.

In summary, **opportunity cost reminds us that every choice has a cost — even if it's not a direct financial one.**<|im_end|>
