The difference between **weather** and **climate** lies in the **time scale** and **scope** of the atmospheric conditions being described:

1. **Weather**:
   - Refers to the **short-term** atmospheric conditions in a specific place at a specific time.
   - It includes variables like temperature, humidity, precipitation, wind speed, and cloud cover.
   - Weather can change **from hour to hour or day to day**.
   - Example: "It's raining today and the temperature is 15°C."

2. **Climate**:
   - Refers to the **long-term** average of weather patterns in a region over a period of time, typically **30 years or more**.
   - It describes the typical or expected weather conditions, including seasonal variations.
   - Climate is more stable and less variable than weather.
   - Example: "The Mediterranean region has a hot, dry summer and mild, wet winter climate."

**Analogy**:
- Weather is like your **mood today** (it can change quickly).
- Climate is like your **personality** (it reflects long-term tendencies).

In summary, **weather is what you're experiencing right now**, while **climate is what you expect based on long-term patterns**
