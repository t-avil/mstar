The time complexity of **quicksort** depends on the choice of pivot and the distribution of the input data:

1. **Best Case**:  
   - When the pivot divides the array into two nearly equal halves.  
   - **Time Complexity**: **O(n log n)**  
   - This occurs when the pivot is always the median or close to it.

2. **Average Case**:  
   - On average, the pivot divides the array into two parts of roughly equal size.  
   - **Time Complexity**: **O(n log n)**  
   - This is the typical performance for random input data.

3. **Worst Case**:  
   - When the pivot is the smallest or largest element every time (e.g., already sorted array with poor pivot selection).  
   - **Time Complexity**: **O(n²)**  
   - This happens when the partitioning is highly unbalanced.

### Summary:
- **Best/Average**: **O(n log n)**  
- **Worst**: **O(n²)**

Quicksort is generally efficient in practice due to its low constant factors and good cache performance, despite its worst-case complexity.<|im_end|>
