Black holes form primarily through the **gravitational collapse of massive stars** at the end of their life cycles. Here's a step-by-step explanation of how this happens:

1. **Massive Star Lifecycle**:
   - Stars much more massive than our Sun (typically more than about **8 times the mass of the Sun**) burn through their nuclear fuel (hydrogen and helium) over millions of years.
   - As the star ages, it fuses heavier elements in its core, eventually forming an iron core.

2. **Core Collapse**:
   - Iron cannot undergo fusion to produce energy, so it no longer supports the star against gravity.
   - Without outward pressure from fusion, the core collapses under its own gravity in a fraction of a second.

3. **Supernova Explosion**:
   - The outer layers of the star rebound off the collapsing core and explode outward in a **supernova**.
   - This explosion can outshine entire galaxies for a short time.

4. **Formation of a Black Hole**:
   - If the remaining core is more than about **3 times the mass of the Sun**, no known force (neutron degeneracy pressure or any other) can stop the collapse.
