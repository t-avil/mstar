The water cycle, also known as the **hydrologic cycle**, is the continuous movement of water on, above, and below the surface of the Earth. It is a natural process that ensures the distribution and recycling of water across the planet. The cycle involves several key processes:

1. **Evaporation**:
   - Water from oceans, rivers, lakes, and other bodies of water is heated by the sun and turns into water vapor (gas).
   - Plants also release water vapor into the air through a process called **transpiration**.
   - Together, evaporation and transpiration are often referred to as **evapotranspiration**.

2. **Condensation**:
   - As water vapor rises into the atmosphere, it cools and changes back into tiny liquid droplets, forming clouds.
   - This process is called **condensation**.

3. **Precipitation**:
   - When the water droplets in clouds become too heavy, they fall back to the Earth as **precipitation**.
   - Precipitation can take various forms, including rain, snow, sleet, or hail.

4. **Collection (or Accumulation)**:
   - Water that falls as precipitation collects in oceans, rivers, lakes, and other surface water
