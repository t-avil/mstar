The terms "democracy" and "republic" are often used interchangeably, but they refer to different concepts—**democracy** describes a system of government based on the will of the people, while **republic** refers to a specific form of government where the head of state is not a monarch and is typically elected.

Here’s a breakdown of the key differences:

### 1. **Definition**
- **Democracy**: A system of government where power is held by the people, either directly or through elected representatives. The core principle is popular sovereignty—rule by the people.
- **Republic**: A form of government in which the country is considered a "public matter" (res publica), not the private concern or property of rulers. The head of state is usually an elected official (like a president), not a hereditary monarch.

### 2. **Focus**
- **Democracy** emphasizes **how decisions are made**—through voting and majority rule.
- **Republic** emphasizes **the structure of government**—specifically, the absence of a monarchy and the idea that the state belongs to the people.
