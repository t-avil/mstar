CRISPR (Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats) is a revolutionary gene-editing technology that allows scientists to precisely modify DNA within living organisms. It is derived from a natural defense mechanism found in bacteria, which they use to protect themselves against viruses.

### How CRISPR Works:

1. **Natural Origin in Bacteria**:
   - Bacteria use CRISPR sequences as part of an adaptive immune system.
   - When a virus infects a bacterium, the bacterium captures a small piece of the viral DNA and inserts it into its own genome within CRISPR arrays.
   - These sequences act as a "memory" of past infections.

2. **CRISPR-Cas9 System**:
   - The most widely used CRISPR tool is **CRISPR-Cas9**, where:
     - **CRISPR** refers to the DNA sequences that store the viral DNA "memories."
     - **Cas9** is an enzyme that acts like molecular scissors, cutting DNA at specific locations.

3. **How Scientists Use It**:
   - Scientists design a **guide RNA (gRNA)** that matches the DNA sequence they want to edit.
   - The gRNA directs the Cas9 enzyme
