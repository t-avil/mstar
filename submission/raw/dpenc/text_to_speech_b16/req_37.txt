The Magna Carta, signed in 1215 by King John of England, is one of the most important legal documents in history. Its significance lies in several key areas:

1. **Foundation of Constitutional Law**: The Magna Carta is often seen as the first step toward constitutional government in England. It established the principle that everyone, including the king, is subject to the law. This was revolutionary at a time when monarchs were considered above the law.

2. **Protection of Rights**: It guaranteed certain rights to the barons (the nobility) and laid the groundwork for the protection of individual liberties. For example, it included clauses that protected the right to a fair trial and prohibited arbitrary imprisonment.

3. **Influence on Future Legal Systems**: The Magna Carta influenced the development of legal systems in England and beyond. It inspired later documents such as the English Bill of Rights (1689), the United States Constitution, and the Universal Declaration of Human Rights.

4. **Limitation of Royal Power**: By forcing the king to acknowledge the rights of his subjects, the Magna Carta curtailed absolute monarchy and helped pave the way for parliamentary democracy.
