The Magna Carta, signed in 1215 by King John of England, is one of the most important documents in the history of constitutional law and the development of democratic principles. Its significance lies in several key areas:

1. **Limitation of Royal Power**: The Magna Carta was the first formal document to limit the power of the English monarchy. It established that the king was not above the law and had to govern according to established legal principles.

2. **Foundation of the Rule of Law**: It introduced the idea that everyone, including the king, is subject to the law. This principle became a cornerstone of modern legal systems.

3. **Protection of Individual Rights**: Although limited in scope at the time, the Magna Carta included provisions that protected certain rights, such as the right to a fair trial and protection from arbitrary imprisonment. Clause 39, for example, stated that no free man could be imprisoned or stripped of his rights except by the lawful judgment of his peers or by the law of the land.
