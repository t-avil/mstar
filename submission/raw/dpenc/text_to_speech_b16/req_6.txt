The **Pythagorean theorem** is a fundamental principle in Euclidean geometry that describes the relationship between the three sides of a right triangle. It states:

> In a right triangle, the square of the length of the hypotenuse (the side opposite the right angle) is equal to the sum of the squares of the lengths of the other two sides (called the legs).

This can be written as the formula:

\[
a^2 + b^2 = c^2
\]

Where:
- \( a \) and \( b \) are the lengths of the two legs,
- \( c \) is the length of the hypotenuse.

### Example:
If a right triangle has legs of lengths 3 and 4, then the hypotenuse \( c \) is:

\[
c = \sqrt{3^2 + 4^2} = \sqrt{9 + 16} = \sqrt{25} = 5
\]

So, the sides are 3, 4, and 5 — a well-known Pythagorean triple.
