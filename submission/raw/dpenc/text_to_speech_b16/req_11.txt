The main causes of World War I can be summarized by the acronym **M.A.I.N.S.**, which stands for:

1. **Militarism**:  
   European powers, especially Germany, Britain, and France, engaged in an arms race and built up large, powerful militaries. This created a climate of competition and tension, where nations believed that military strength was essential for national prestige and security.

2. **Alliances**:  
   A complex web of military alliances divided Europe into two opposing camps:  
   - The **Triple Entente** (France, Russia, and Britain)  
   - The **Triple Alliance** (Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy)  
   These alliances meant that a conflict between two countries could quickly escalate into a war involving multiple nations.

3. **Imperialism**:  
   European powers competed for colonies and global influence, particularly in Africa and Asia. This rivalry heightened tensions, especially between Britain and Germany, as Germany sought to expand its empire and challenge British dominance.
