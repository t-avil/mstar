The main causes of World War I can be summarized by the acronym **M.A.I.N.S.**, which stands for:

1. **Militarism**:  
   European powers, especially Germany, Britain, and France, engaged in an arms race, building up their military forces and naval fleets. This created a climate of competition and tension, where nations believed that war could be won through military strength.

2. **Alliances**:  
   A complex web of military alliances divided Europe into two opposing camps:  
   - The **Triple Entente**: France, Russia, and Britain.  
   - The **Triple Alliance**: Germany, Austria-Hungary, and Italy (though Italy later switched sides).  
   These alliances meant that a conflict between two countries could quickly escalate into a continent-wide war.

3. **Imperialism**:  
   European powers competed for colonies and global influence, particularly in Africa and Asia. This rivalry heightened tensions, especially between Britain and Germany, which was rapidly expanding its empire.

4. **Nationalism**:  
   Intense national pride and desire for independence fueled tensions. In the Balkans, Slavic nationalism threatened the Austro-Hungarian Empire, while in Serbia, nationalism supported the idea of uniting all South Slavs.
