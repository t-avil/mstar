*1984* is a dystopian novel by George Orwell, published in 1949. The story is set in a totalitarian society ruled by the Party, which exercises total control over every aspect of life in the fictional superstate of Oceania. The protagonist, Winston Smith, is a low-ranking member of the Party who works at the Ministry of Truth, where he alters historical records to align with the Party’s ever-changing narratives.

Winston harbors secret rebellious thoughts and begins to question the Party’s authority. He starts a forbidden romantic relationship with Julia, a woman who also opposes the Party. Together, they seek freedom and truth, believing they can find refuge in the countryside or through the Brotherhood, a rumored underground resistance movement.

However, their rebellion is short-lived. They are betrayed and captured by the Thought Police. Winston is subjected to intense psychological and physical torture in the Ministry of Love, where he is forced to renounce his love for Julia and fully submit to the Party’s ideology. Ultimately, he is broken and learns to love Big Brother, the Party’s omnipresent leader.

The novel explores themes of totalitarianism, surveillance, propaganda, and the manipulation of truth and history. It serves as a warning about the dangers of unchecked political power
