Bread rises during baking primarily due to the action of **yeast** and the **heat** applied during the process. Here's a step-by-step explanation of how this happens:

1. **Yeast Fermentation (Before Baking):**  
   When you mix yeast with flour, water, and sugar, the yeast becomes active. It consumes the sugars in the dough and converts them into **carbon dioxide (CO₂)** and **alcohol** through a process called **fermentation**. The CO₂ gets trapped in the gluten network of the dough, causing it to expand and rise. This is known as **proofing** or **rising**.

2. **Heat During Baking:**  
   When the bread is placed in a hot oven, several things happen:
   - The **yeast becomes very active** at first, producing more CO₂ until it gets too hot and dies (usually around 130–140°F or 55–60°C).
   - The **trapped CO₂ expands** due to the heat, causing the dough to rise further. This is called **oven spring**.
