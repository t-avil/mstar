The wind off the North Sea had a different quality tonight—thicker, hungrier. Elias Thorne adjusted the collar of his oilskin coat, the damp wool already clinging to his neck. He’d been tending the lantern at the top of Blackrock Point for twenty-three years, and he knew the sea’s moods like the lines on his own hands. But this… this felt like the calm before the storm that never quite broke.

He climbed the spiral stairs, each step echoing in the stone tower. The great Fresnel lens stood sentinel in the lantern room, its prisms catching the last of the dying light and refracting it into a thousand shards of silver across the churning water. Elias checked the oil reservoir, the clockwork mechanism, the wick. All as it should be. Routine. Safety.
