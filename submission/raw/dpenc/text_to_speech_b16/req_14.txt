The wind off the North Sea had a different quality tonight—thicker, hungrier. Elias Thorne adjusted the collar of his oilskin coat, the damp wool already clinging to his neck. He’d been tending the lantern at the top of the Blackwater Point Lighthouse for twenty-three years, and he knew the sea’s moods like the lines on his own hands. But this… this felt like the calm before the storm that would swallow the world whole.

He climbed the spiral stairs, each step groaning beneath his boots, the iron railing cold even through his gloves. The great Fresnel lens stood sentinel in the lantern room, its prisms catching the last of the dying light and refracting it into a thousand shards of silver across the churning water below. Elias checked the oil reservoir, the wick, the clockwork mechanism that turned the lens every thirty seconds. All as it should be. Yet, as he wiped a smudge from the glass with his sleeve, he couldn’t shake the feeling that something was… off.
