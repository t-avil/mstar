The way newspapers would report on an event depends heavily on the nature of the event itself, the political and cultural context, the newspaper's editorial stance, and the target audience. Here are some general ways different types of papers might cover a story:

1. **Mainstream Newspapers (e.g., The New York Times, The Guardian):**
   - **Objective and Balanced:** They typically aim for factual reporting with multiple perspectives.
   - **In-Depth Analysis:** May include background information, expert opinions, and historical context.
   - **Investigative Reporting:** If the story involves wrongdoing or complex issues, they might publish investigative pieces.
   - **Editorials and Opinions:** Separate from news reporting, these sections may offer commentary from columnists.

2. **Tabloid Newspapers (e.g., The Sun, Daily Mail):**
   - **Sensationalism:** Often use dramatic headlines and focus on emotional or controversial aspects.
   - **Simplified Language:** Aim to appeal to a broad audience with straightforward language.
   - **Focus on Human Interest:** May emphasize personal stories or scandals.
